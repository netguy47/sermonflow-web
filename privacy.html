1. Summary

Sermon Flow is a note-taking app for people who write sermons. It stores the sermon notes you write, on your behalf, so that they are available on your devices. It also offers an optional AI-assisted drafting tool that helps you start a sermon outline. That is the whole of the data relationship.

In short:

  • We do not collect personal information.
  • We do not use your data for tracking.
  • We do not sell or rent your data to anyone.
  • We do not use third-party analytics or advertising, and the app contains no advertising SDKs.
  • We do not ask you to create an account, and there is no login screen, email address, or password.
  • The AI drafting feature is optional and off until you ask for it. Scripture text is bundled with the app; only the short text you type into the Create tab is ever sent to an AI provider.

It detects scripture references in your text and shows the passage from a Bible translation bundled inside the app. It can also show your most recent note on your Home Screen widget. Separately, the Create tab can ask an AI provider to draft a sermon outline from a passage, topic and instructions you type — see Section 4.

4. AI-Assisted Sermon Drafting (optional)

The Create tab can draft a sermon outline for you. This feature is optional: everything else in the app — writing notes, saving them, searching, organising, and the widget — works normally whether or not you use it, and whether or not the AI service is available.

Who handles the request

When you tap Generate Outline, the app first shows a disclosure sheet naming the provider. The request goes to our own drafting service running on Google Cloud (Firebase Cloud Functions), and that service calls Google Gemini through the Google AI API. The provider's access credential is held in Google Cloud's secret manager and is never contained in, or reachable from, the app that is distributed through the App Store.

Before you submit, the app tells you which provider will handle the request and exactly what will be transmitted.

Exactly what is sent

Only the three fields you type in the Create tab are sent:

  • the passage you entered,
  • the topic you entered,
  • the instructions you entered.

Nothing else is transmitted. Specifically, the app does not send your sermon notes, your anonymous account identifier, any email address, any other Firestore content, your device information, your name, or your payment information. The drafting service rejects any request containing additional fields, so this limit is enforced on the server and not only in the app.

How the provider processes it

The text you submitted is transmitted to Google Gemini, which processes it under Google's own terms and privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy). We do not use your submitted text to train our own models, we do not add it to your notes automatically, and we do not sell it. The draft comes back to the app for you to read.

You can avoid sending anything

You choose whether to use the feature. Nothing is sent unless you tap Generate Outline and confirm the disclosure sheet. If you would rather not use AI drafting at all, simply do not use the Create tab's Generate button — no text leaves your device, and the rest of the app is unaffected.

The output is a draft, not Scripture

Every AI-generated outline is labelled "AI-generated draft—review Scripture references and theological claims." The outline is a starting point produced by a machine. It is not Scripture, it is not a quotation, and it may be inaccurate. Verify every Scripture reference and every theological claim against the Bible and your own sources before you preach it. The drafting service instructs the model not to quote Scripture text and not to present any wording as the words of Scripture, and the app adds no quotations, citations or scholarly sources of its own — but that is a prompt-level instruction, not a guarantee, and no automated check can confirm every reference in free-form text. You should not present any part of the draft as a quotation without checking it yourself.

If the service is unavailable

If the drafting service is unreachable, unconfigured, or declines the request, the app says so plainly and shows no outline at all. It never substitutes a sample or invented outline. Your notes and the rest of the app continue to work.

6. Anonymous Authentication, and Why Your Notes Are Private

The app signs you in to Firebase using anonymous authentication. When the app first runs, it creates a random, non-identifying account tied to this installation and uses that account as the key under which your notes are stored. No identity information is attached to it, and there is no account screen, sign-up, or sign-in anywhere in the app.

The practical consequences, stated plainly:

  • Your notes are stored against a randomly generated identifier, not against you as a person.
  • Your notes are not visible to other users of the app, and the app has no feature for sharing notes with other users.
  • We do not have a name, an email address, or a phone number against which to identify the owner of a set of notes, and we do not ask you for one.
  • Because the identifier is anonymous and device-scoped, uninstalling the app or deleting it from your device means we may have no way to associate those notes with you. See Section 10 (Data Rights).

How your notes are kept separate from everyone else's

The app's Firestore database enforces the separation on the server, not only in the app. Every note document records the anonymous account identifier that owns it, and the database's security rules allow a request only when the identifier on the request matches the identifier stored on the document. The rules also require that a query name the identifier being read — the app's own note list is filtered on it — so a request that tries to list every note in the database is rejected by the server rather than filtered afterwards. A signed-out request is rejected outright.

The practical effect: a note written on one installation is not readable, listable, editable or deletable from any other installation, and the app itself never requests notes it does not own. This is enforced by the database, so it holds even if the app is modified or a bug is introduced.

7. Local Notifications

The app asks for permission to send you notifications the first time it launches. This is a request for the standard iOS alert, sound, and badge permission, handled entirely by iOS on your device. The app does not use a remote push service: it has no push-notification backend and does not receive push messages from us or from anyone else. If you grant the permission, the app can schedule local reminders on your device. A Monday Recall reminder, which you switch on individually for a note, repeats every Monday at 9:00 AM local time on that device. You can switch it off again on the same note, and deleting the note removes its reminder as well, so a reminder never outlives the note it refers to. Reminders carry the note's title and the opening of its body text, on your device only. You can decline the prompt, change your mind later in iOS Settings → Notifications → Sermon Flow, and turn individual reminder types off. Declining the prompt does not reduce any feature of the app; it only stops reminders.

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, export, or delete the personal data we hold about you, and to object to or restrict certain processing.

In practice, for this app:

Because we hold no identity information for you, we can only answer an access request by working from the anonymous account identifier, and cannot map that identifier to a person.
  • Correction. Edit or update a note at any time by opening it from the timeline and saving your changes.
  • Deletion. You can delete any note yourself, at any time, from the app: open the note and choose Delete Note, or swipe the note in the timeline and choose Delete. This removes the note from cloud storage and from this device immediately, including when the app is on the free tier and the five-note limit is reached — the limit restricts new notes, never deletion. Deleting a note also removes any Monday reminder attached to it. 11. Data Retention

Your notes are retained in cloud storage for as long as you keep using the app, and until you request their deletion or your anonymous account is removed by Google. On-device data (the shared app-group copy of your most recent note, and the app's local settings) is removed when you delete the app, and the shared copy of a note is also removed as soon as you delete that note in the app. Notification permission state and any scheduled reminders are managed by iOS on your device. Text you send to the AI drafting service is not retained by us beyond generating and returning your draft; any retention by the AI provider is governed by that provider's own terms.
